# Personal voice — auditable profile (direct-assertion register, realigned 2026-08-11)

The individual's own name and page: posts, essays, op-eds, comments, replies. Generic
bundled default: if the org config names a source voice skill or overrides rules, the
config and that skill take precedence on any divergence — this file is the default for
orgs without one. Every line here is a rule the audit checks, not a vibe.

## Posture (T1)

- Settled. No race, no clock, nothing urgent. The author states what they see and what
  they do, plainly, and stops when that is done.
- Never afraid ("AI will replace us"), never triumphant ("here's the secret"), never
  hand-wringing ("I'm still figuring it out"). Humble and settled at once: unimpressed
  by the own work, more interested in the idea than the author.
- The byline carries the person; the sentences carry the subject. First-person singular
  is effectively absent from this voice. "We" and "our" appear only when stating
  organizational practice as fact ("In our own operations, nothing external ships
  without a human gate.").
- Allowed to be: generous, quiet, a little dry (mild understatement yes, sarcasm no),
  opinionated, with every view asserted plainly about the subject, no hedging, no
  self-reference.

## Shape (audit long-form against this)

1. **The fact.** Open on the thing itself: a law with its article number and date, an
   event, a shipped capability, a documented case. No opener about what the world thinks.
2. **The analysis, asserted.** What the fact means, stated as a direct professional
   assertion about the named subject: "Article 5 compliance starts as an inventory
   question." / "The exemption carries the design." The byline owns the analysis; the
   sentence asserts it. One analytical line of argument per piece, scoped to the
   subject. General truths about the world stay banned.
3. **The anchor.** The law's own text, a shipped capability, an actual practice, real
   history. No floating theory, no invented conversations, no dramatized details.
4. **The closing line.** Short, quiet, owned. Never a CTA, never a question to the
   audience, never a moral for the reader to adopt.

## Mechanical rules (T0-grade, one hit = BLOCK, this voice only)

- **No first-person hedging (T0-HEDGE).** "For me", "to me", "I think", "I believe",
  "I suspect", "I keep thinking about", "I keep coming back to", "I keep arriving at",
  "in my view", "Personally,", "My take is", "My sense is", "reads to us like", and
  ANY equivalent singular self-referential framing of analysis — the list narrows,
  the definition decides. Analysis is asserted directly about the named subject; the
  byline owns it.
- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Commas, colons, and periods carry the rhythm.
- No emojis. No hashtags, anywhere, on personal surfaces.
- No CTAs: no "follow me", "subscribe", "DM me", "what do you think?".
- No links in the body; a required link goes in the first comment.
- No headers in a LinkedIn post. No bullet blocks unless the content is truly a list;
  more than one bullet block means rewrite as prose.
- X: never 1/n threads. An X Article carries the full piece; one long-form post carries
  the compressed version.
- Plus any lexicon bans the org config adds for this voice (config wins).

## Register rules (T1)

- No predictions: no future dates, no percentages, no "in five years". Talk about what is.
- No advice, no imperatives at the reader: state the observation about the subject.
  A line that sounds like advice softens to observation or dies.
- No competitor names. Ever.
- Concrete: name the actual thing, an article number, a document, a mechanism.
- Short paragraphs, often one or two sentences. White space is part of the voice.
- Cleverness is a yellow flag: settled beats sharp.
- Dinner-table test: a line that would sound like a brochure said out loud gets rewritten.
- No fearmongering, no hype: both ends of that spectrum belong to people selling
  something, and this voice sells nothing.
- Product mentions: at most one per piece, indirect, no CTA (see config claims policy).

## Lexicon

Avoid: game-changer, unlock, leverage, synergy, ecosystem, paradigm shift, journey,
transform, disrupt, revolutionize, next-gen, cutting-edge, AI-powered, best-in-class,
world-class, "I'm excited to announce", "I'm thrilled", "I'm humbled", "in today's
fast-paced world", "in an era of", "the landscape is changing", "hot take",
"unpopular opinion", "let me be clear", "we need to figure out", "the time is now",
and any sweeping "most X" generalization. The org config may add or remove words;
the config wins.

Use: direct assertions scoped to the named subject: "X starts as…", "X carries the
design", "X sits there", "The article asks for…"; organizational practice as fact:
"In our own operations…"; evaluative assertions about the subject, asserted once:
"The honest part of the text sits there: …"; short factual closers: "In the end the
article checks for one thing: …".

## Length and format

- LinkedIn personal post: 150 to 350 words. Hook on line 1. Three to six short
  paragraphs. Closing line on its own.
- Essay / op-ed: 600 to 1,200 words, same shape, no headers unless structurally needed.
- X: Article (full piece) plus one long-form post. Comment/reply: 1 to 3 sentences,
  same posture.
- The config surface table overrides these bounds where it differs.

## Config hooks

- Anchor touchpoints (real org facts used to ground the analysis) come from the config
  overlay; they are stated as facts, never packaged as morals.
- The config may replace this profile with its own.
